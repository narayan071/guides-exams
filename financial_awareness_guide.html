Ch 1: RBI, Subsidiaries, Currency & Financial Regulators High-Yield

Start Here The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is India's central bank, banker to the Central Government, banker to scheduled commercial banks, and Lender of Last Resort. Know its Governor, offices, 5 subsidiaries, currency printing facts, and sister regulators (SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA, NABARD, SIDBI, NHB).

RBI Core Facts & Currency

  • Act & Founding: Established under RBI Act, 1934 (also lays down legal provisions for foreign exchange reserves); started 1 April 1935; nationalized in 1949.
  • Governors: 1st Governor = Sir Osborne Smith | 1st Indian Governor = C. D. Deshmukh | Current (26th) = Sanjay Malhotra (Dec 11, 2024).
  • Offices & Board: Central Office shifted from Kolkata to Mumbai in 1937. 4 Zonal Offices (Chennai, Kolkata, Delhi, Mumbai), 21 Regional Offices, 11 Sub-Offices. Board: 1 Governor, 4 Deputy Governors, 15 Directors. Reserve Bank Staff College is at Chennai.
  • Currency Facts: - ₹1 Note is printed by the Government of India (Ministry of Finance). - Intaglio Printing: Technique used for raised printing of "Reserve Bank of India" on notes. - Most preferred currency note (RBI survey): ₹100. - Soiled notes: Dirty or slightly cut notes. Mutilated notes: Torn, cut, or missing essential pieces. - Decimal System of Coinage (1957): Moved from Anna system (1 Rupee = 16 Anna = 64 Pice = 192 Pies) to 1 Rupee = 100 Naya Paisa ("Naya" dropped in 1964).

Ch 2: Banking Structure, Commercial Bank Functions & History Updated Limits

Start Here Understand commercial bank functions (Primary vs Agency vs General Utility), scheduled vs non-scheduled banks, historic bank mergers/firsts, and differentiated bank rules (Payments Banks & SFBs).

Commercial Bank Functions

  • Primary Functions: Receive deposits, lend loans, create credit.
  • Secondary (Agency) Functions: Transfer funds, collect cheques/dividends, pay bills/insurance on standing instructions.
  • Secondary (General Utility) Functions: Safe deposit lockers, Letter of Credit (LC), foreign exchange services.

Ch 3: Monetary Policy (63rd MPC), Ratios, MCLR, Inflation & Fiscal Terms

Start Here The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) sets policy rates (SDF ↔ Repo Rate ↔ MSF) while RBI manages reserve ratios (CRR ↔ SLR). Repo rate is inversely proportional to inflation (Central Bank raises Repo Rate to control inflation).

RBI Policy Rates, Ratios & Lending Benchmarks (Oct/Nov 2026)

ToolDefinition & RuleCurrent Value
Repo RateRate at which RBI lends short-term to commercial banks against govt securities. 63rd MPC Stance: Calibrated Tightening (from Neutral).5.50% (prev 5.25%)
SDF (Standing Deposit Facility)Floor of LAF corridor (25 bps below Repo).5.25% (-0.25%)
MSF (Marginal Standing Facility)Emergency overnight borrowing window by RBI for SCBs (25 bps above Repo).5.75% (+0.25%)
Bank RateRate at which RBI is ready to buy or rediscount bills of exchange or commercial paper.5.75%
Reverse Repo RateRate at which banks park surplus with RBI; absorbs excess liquidity (high Reverse Repo → less lending → low money supply).3.35%
CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio)% of bank's NDTL (Net Demand & Time Liabilities) kept in liquid cash with RBI. Daily maintenance: 99% of required CRR in a fortnight (w.e.f. 16 Oct 2026, earlier 90%).3.00%
SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio)Reserve under Section 24 of Banking Regulation Act, 1949; held internally by bank in cash, gold, or approved securities (securities earn interest).18.00%
WMA (Ways & Means Advances)Max short-term loan govt can borrow from RBI (at Repo Rate) for temporary receipts/payments mismatch.₹50,000 crore (H2 limit)
MCLRMarginal Cost of Funds-Based Lending Rate (introduced 1 April 2016) — minimum lending rate below which a bank is not allowed to lend.Bank-specific
OMO (Open Market Operations)RBI primarily buys and sells government securities to manage liquidity.Liquidity tool
LRR (Legal Reserve Ratio)Minimum fraction of deposits banks are legally required to keep as reserves (combines CRR + SLR).CRR + SLR
LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio)Ensures bank holds enough High-Quality Liquid Assets (cash, govt securities) to survive an acute 30-day cash outflow stress scenario (Basel III).30-day stress
NSFR (Net Stable Funding Ratio)Ensures bank maintains a stable funding profile relative to off-balance-sheet assets & activities over a 1-year horizon (Basel III).1-year horizon

Inflation & Fiscal Policy Terms

TermDefinition / Mechanism
Inflation / DeflationInflation = rise in prices due to currency devaluation (Demand-Pull, Cost-Push, Built-in: wage ↑ → cost ↑). Deflation = price decline.
Stagflation / GallopingStagflation = slow growth + high unemployment + high inflation. Galloping = double/triple-digit inflation.
Fiscal Push / Fiscal DragFiscal Push: govt actions (e.g., low tax) to boost demand & growth. Fiscal Drag: slowdown when rising incomes/inflation push taxpayers into higher tax brackets.
Austerity MeasuresStrict govt cost-cutting & tax increases to shrink deficits & public debt (cools inflation but slows short-term growth).
Capex vs OpexCapex: capital expenditure on long-term assets (roads, railways). Opex: daily running/operational costs.
Lockboxes (Post Lockboxes)Most common corporate cash management technique.

Quick Check

1. Under which section of the Banking Regulation Act 1949 is SLR mandated?Section 24 (currently 18%, held internally in cash, gold, or approved securities).
2. What is the difference between Fiscal Push and Fiscal Drag?Fiscal Push boosts demand via lower taxes/spending; Fiscal Drag slows demand as rising incomes push taxpayers into higher tax brackets.
3. When was MCLR introduced and what does it represent?April 1, 2016; minimum lending rate below which a bank cannot lend.
4. What is the H2 WMA limit and at what rate is it offered?₹50,000 crore, offered at the Repo Rate.

Start Here Money Market (high liquidity, ≤ 1 yr) includes T-Bills, CP, CD, CMB, Call/Notice Money. Capital Market (> 1 yr) splits into Primary (IPO) and Secondary (Stock Exchanges). Also know the 4 investment flows (FDI, Indirect, Downstream, Upstream).

Capital Market & Investment Flows

  • Primary vs Secondary Market: Primary = new issues (IPO); Secondary = trading previously issued securities (Stock Exchanges). Depositories under SEBI: NSDL (National Securities Depository Ltd) & CDSL (Central Depository Services Ltd).
  • FDI (Foreign Direct Investment): Foreign company invests directly into an Indian company.
  • Indirect Investment: Foreign money enters an Indian company indirectly through another Indian entity owned by foreigners.
  • Downstream Investment: Investment by one Indian company in another Indian company.
  • Upstream Investment: When a subsidiary company invests back up into its parent company.

Quick Check

1. What is the difference between Downstream and Upstream investment?Downstream = one Indian company investing in another Indian company; Upstream = subsidiary investing back up into its parent company.
2. Name the two depositories registered with SEBI.NSDL and CDSL.

Cheques, MICR, Demand Draft & Cards

  • Cheque Types (Section 6, NI Act 1881 covers cheques, electronic & truncated cheques): - Open Cheque: Not crossed (can be encashed at counter). - Crossed Cheque: Two parallel lines (//) on top-left; deposited in bank account only, not encashed. - Order Cheque: Payable to the specific person named on it. - Stale Cheque: Presented after 3 months from issue date (note: old 6-month rule was changed by RBI to 3 months).
  • MICR Code (9 digits at bottom of cheque): First 3 = City | Middle 3 = Bank Code | Last 3 = Branch Code.
  • Demand Draft (Banker's Cheque): Valid for 3 months; cash limit < ₹50,000 (≥ ₹50,000 via cheque + PAN).
  • Cards & Terms: Debit Card = 16 digits | CVV = Card Verification Value (proves physical possession) | EMV = Europay, Mastercard, Visa | MDR = Merchant Discount Rate | Charge Card (pay in full or pay fees).

Ch 6: NPCI, Digital Payments, SWIFT & ATMs 24×7 & ₹5L Updated

Start Here NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India, est. 19 Dec 2008) operates India's retail payment rails, while NEFT & RTGS are managed directly by RBI.

NPCI Products vs RBI Systems

  • Managed by NPCI: UPI, AePS (Aadhaar Enabled Payment System — uses Aadhaar for authentication), BHIM (launched 30 Dec 2016 by PM Modi; supports 20 languages), IMPS (uses 7-digit MMID — Mobile Money Identifier), *99# (USSD basic mobile banking without internet), RuPay, NFS (National Financial Switch — ATM network), NETC / FASTag (National Electronic Toll Collection), BBPS / Bharat Connect (recurrent bill payments), NACH (National Automated Clearing House), CTS (Cheque Truncation System), e-RUPI.
  • Managed directly by RBI (NOT NPCI): NEFT, RTGS, CBDC (Central Bank Digital Currency), ADF (Automated Data Flow).
  • CBS (Core Banking Solution): Allows customers to access accounts and banking services across interconnected branches.
  • ECS (Electronic Clearing Service): Used for bulk payments (salaries, dividends, utility bills); succeeded by NACH.
  • SWIFT: Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication — international money transfer messaging.

  • Brown Label ATM: Hardware/lease by 3rd party, while cash management & branding are maintained by the bank.

NPA, Banking Risks, Narasimham Committee & Frauds

  • NPA (Non-Performing Asset): Loan/advance for which principal or interest payment is overdue for 90+ days.
  • Banking Risk Types: Market Risk, Liquidity Risk, Systematic Risk, Reputational Risk.
  • Narasimham Committee: Recommended Banking Structure Reforms.
  • Banking Cyber Frauds: - Skimming: Fraudsters install a skimmer device on ATMs/gas pumps to steal card data. - Phishing: Fake emails/websites to steal credentials. - Smishing: Phishing via SMS. - Spoofing: Disguising communication (Caller ID / IP / email) to appear legitimate. - SIM Swap: Fraudster duplicates victim's SIM card to hijack OTPs.

Ch 8: Banking Ombudsman, NARCL & Global Institutions RB-IOS Update

  • Integrated Ombudsman Scheme – 2026 (Effective June 1, 2026; replaces 2021 scheme): - Motto: "One Nation, One Ombudsman"; statutory base: Section 35A, BR Act 1949 (orig. 1995 → 2002 → 2006 → 2021 → 2026). Financial loss is not the only criteria; No fee for filing. - Tenure & Offices: RBI Ombudsman & Deputy Ombudsman tenure = 3 years; 21 Regional Offices. - Covered Entities: All SCBs, RRBs, all Cooperative Banks (non-scheduled Primary UCBs if deposit size ≥ ₹50 Cr), customer-interface NBFCs (asset size > ₹100 Cr) & deposit-taking NBFCs, Non-bank PPI issuers, and Credit Information Companies (CICs) (newly covered). Excluded: Housing Finance Companies (HFCs). - Filing & Timelines: First complain to Regulated Entity (within Limitation Act, 1963 period) → wait > 30 days (or unsatisfactory/no response) → file with RBI Ombudsman within 90 days from waiting period expiry. File personally or via authorised representative (Advocate normally NOT authorised unless personally affected). - Channels: Online via CMS (Complaint Management System); Email/Physical processed at CRPC (Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre, Chandigarh). NOT accepted via RBI Contact Centre. - Exclusions: Already in court proceedings; Vendor–RE dispute; Employee–Employer dispute; Dispute between REs; incomplete/abusive/frivolous complaints. - Proceedings & Awards: Nature = Summary proceedings. RE gets 15 days for written response + docs (on failure, RBI proceeds ex-parte). Outcomes: Settlement, Award, or Rejection. Underlying dispute value: No limit; Max compensation for consequential loss = ₹30 Lakh + additional ₹3 Lakh for mental harassment. Complainant must accept award within 30 days (else lapses). - Appeals & Compliance: Appeal within 30 days max to Appellate Authority = Executive Director in charge of CEPD (Consumer Education & Protection Dept.). RE cannot appeal if it failed to furnish required info/docs. Principal Nodal Officer at RE head office must be min rank General Manager or equivalent. Salient features displayed at branches in English, Hindi, and Regional language.
  • NARCL (National Asset Reconstruction Company Ltd): India's Bad Bank; takes over and manages stressed assets (bad loans) from banks.
  • IMF (International Monetary Fund): Resolves Balance of Payments issues worldwide; est. 27 Dec 1945; HQ: Washington, D.C.
  • World Bank: Formally IBRD (International Bank for Reconstruction and Development).
  • NDB (New Development Bank / BRICS Bank): First President = K. V. Kamath.
