Ch 1: RBI, Subsidiaries, Currency & Financial Regulators High-Yield

Start Here The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is India's central bank, banker to the Central Government, banker to scheduled commercial banks, and Lender of Last Resort. Know its Governor, offices, 5 subsidiaries, currency printing facts, and sister regulators (SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA, NABARD, SIDBI, NHB).

RBI Core Facts & Currency

  • Act, Scope & Trap: Established under RBI Act, 1934 (Second Schedule = Scheduled Banks; governs foreign exchange reserves & NBFCs); started 1 April 1935; nationalized 1 Jan 1949. Exam Trap: RBI does NOT decide individual banks' Prime Lending Rate / Base Rate.
  • Governors & 5C Strategy: 1st = Sir Osborne Smith | 1st Indian = C. D. Deshmukh | 26th (Current) = Sanjay Malhotra. RBI 5C Financial Literacy: Content, Capacity, Community, Communication, Collaboration.
  • Offices & Portals: HQ shifted Kolkata → Mumbai (1937); 4 Zonal, 21 Regional, 11 Sub-Offices; Board = 1 Gov + 4 Dy Govs + 15 Directors; Staff College = Chennai. Portals: UDGAM (search unclaimed deposits across banks) & PRAVAAH (online regulatory approvals).
  • Currency Facts:
    • Max Denominations: Banknotes up to ₹10,000 (Sec 24, RBI Act); Coins up to ₹1,000 (Coinage Act 2011).
    • ₹1 Note & Coins: Issued by Govt of India (MoF); higher notes by RBI. Coin distribution incentive for banks = ₹65/bag (hiked from ₹25).
    • Intaglio Printing: Technique used for raised printing of "Reserve Bank of India" on notes.
    • Most preferred currency note (RBI survey): ₹100.
    • Soiled vs Mutilated notes: Soiled = dirty or slightly cut; Mutilated = torn, cut, or missing essential pieces.
    • Decimal System of Coinage (1957): Moved from Anna system (1 Rupee = 16 Anna = 64 Pice = 192 Pies) to 1 Rupee = 100 Naya Paisa ("Naya" dropped in 1964).

Commercial Bank Functions

  • Primary Functions: Receive deposits, lend loans, create credit.
  • Secondary (Agency) Functions: Transfer funds, collect cheques/dividends, pay bills/insurance on standing instructions.
  • Secondary (General Utility) Functions: Safe deposit lockers, Letter of Credit (LC), foreign exchange services.

MPC Rules, Qualitative Tools, Inflation & Macro (GDP / Crypto)

MPC Structure & Stances

  • Setup (2016): Amended RBI Act 1934; 6 members = 3 RBI (Governor as Chair, Dy. Governor, 1 officer) + 3 external (appointed by Govt for 4 yrs).
  • Meetings (Trap!): Act mandates at least 4/year; RBI holds 6 (bi-monthly). Quorum = 4; Governor has casting vote.
  • Stances & Tools: Accommodative, Neutral, Withdrawal / Calibrated tightening. 1 bps = 0.01% (100 bps = 1%). RBI joined NGFS (Network for Greening the Financial System) in 2021.
  • FBIL Benchmarks & Qualitative: Significant benchmarks by FBIL: MIBOR, MIFOR, MMIFOR, USD/INR Reference Rate, T-Bill, G-Sec & SDL Valuation. Qualitative = moral suasion, margin norms.

Inflation Indices & Target

  • FIT (2016): Target 4% ± 2% (band 2%–6%), renewed March 2026 for 2026–31. Outside band for 3 straight quarters → RBI must explain to Govt.
  • CPI (MPC Target): Monthly household prices by NSO (MoSPI). WPI: Wholesale prices by Economic Adviser, DPIIT.
  • Core Inflation: Excludes food & fuel. Disinflation: Prices rising slower (vs Deflation = falling; Stagflation = high inflation + slow growth; Galloping = 2–3 digit).

GDP, GVA & Crypto / VDA

  • GDP, Inflation & Growth (FY 2026–27): Real GDP (inflation-adjusted) vs Nominal; GVA = GDP − taxes + subsidies. RBI Oct 2026 FY27 projections: Real GDP = 7.1% (Q1 actual 7.8%; Q2 FY26 = 8.2%) & CPI = 5.2% (Core 4.4%).
  • Union Budget 2026–27 (1 Feb 2026): Fiscal Deficit BE = 4.3% of GDP (vs 4.4% RE FY26); Debt-to-GDP = 55.6%; Public Capex = ₹12.2 lakh crore (up from ₹11.2L Cr); Gross/Net Market Borrowings = ₹17.2L Cr / ₹11.7L Cr.
  • Bitcoin & VDA: Created 2009 on blockchain; Max supply = 21 million. India: 30% tax + 1% TDS on VDAs (not RBI legal tender).

Capital Market, ASBA, Share Issues & G-Secs

ASBA, Exchanges & G-Secs

  • ASBA & SCSB: Application Supported by Blocked Amount (SEBI, 2008) — blocks IPO money until allotment via an SCSB (Self Certified Syndicate Bank; retail UPI mandate supported).
  • Exchanges, Funds & Indices: BSE (Sensex), NSE (Nifty 50), Depositories NSDL & CDSL, Settlement = T+1. Mutual Funds: Equity, Debt, Hybrid (mixed), ELSS (3-yr lock-in, Sec 80C), NAV. Global Indices: Hang Seng (HK), Nikkei 225 (Japan), FTSE 100 (UK), DAX (Germany), Dow Jones / S&P 500 (US).
  • G-Secs: Lending to government (zero default risk). Dated G-Secs (5–40 yrs), SDLs (state bonds). Bought via RBI Retail Direct (12 Nov 2021).

Types of Share Issues

  • IPO: First-time public share sale by unlisted company (Primary Market).
  • FPO: Already-listed company issues new shares to public.
  • Rights Issue: New shares offered to existing shareholders (usually discounted).
  • OFS (Offer for Sale): Promoters/holders sell existing shares (money goes to sellers, NOT company).
  • QIP / Private Placement / Bonus: QIP = quick sale to big institutions; Private = small chosen group; Bonus = free shares from reserves.

Foreign Exchange, LRS & Investment Flows

  • LRS, Forex Cards & Accounts: LRS (FEMA 1999): Resident individuals up to USD 250,000/FY. EEFC (Exchange Earners' Foreign Currency) = non-interest (0%) foreign currency account in India. FFMC = Full-Fledged Money Changer (FEMA Sec 10). Forex Prepaid / Travel Cards: charges payable in India must be denominated & cleared in INR (Rupees). IFSCA HQ: GIFT City, Gandhinagar.
  • FDI vs FPI vs ECB: FDI = lasting stake/control directly into an Indian firm | FPI = Foreign Portfolio Investment (shares/bonds without control) | ECB = External Commercial Borrowing (foreign loan raised by Indian firm).
  • Indirect / Downstream / Upstream: Indirect = foreign money via another foreign-owned Indian entity | Downstream = Indian company investing in another Indian company | Upstream = subsidiary investing back into its parent.

Cheques, Negotiable Instruments, Demand Draft & Cards

NI Act 1881, Parties & Cheque Types

  • 3 Negotiable Instruments (NI Act 1881): Cheque (Sec 6 incl. electronic/truncated), Bill of Exchange (creditor orders debtor), Promissory Note (debtor promises to pay).
  • 3 Parties: Drawer (writes cheque), Drawee (paying bank), Payee (receiver).
  • Types: Bearer (payable to holder) | Order (named payee or endorsed) | Open (cash at counter) | Crossed (bank account only) | Self (withdraw own cash).
  • Dates & Siblings: Valid 3 months (since 1 Apr 2012) → Stale after 3m | Post-dated (future) | Antedated (past, valid ≤ 3m) | Mutilated (needs drawer confirmation). Traveller's cheque = prepaid, redeemable abroad; Banker's cheque / Pay order = drawn by bank on itself (same city).

4 Crossings, Endorsements & Bounce (Sec 138)

  • General Crossing (// or "& Co."): Collected through any bank account (cannot encash at counter).
  • Special Crossing: Bank name written across lines → collected ONLY via that named bank.
  • "Account Payee" Crossing: Credited ONLY to payee's account; cannot be endorsed/transferred.
  • "Not Negotiable" (Sec 130): Transferable, but new holder gets no better title than transferor.
  • 4 Endorsements (back sign): Blank (becomes bearer), Special/Full (names new payee), Restrictive ("Pay X only"), Conditional.
  • Dishonour (Sec 138 NI Act): Up to 2 yrs jail or 2× amount fine or both. Process: Notice within 30d of return memo → 15d to pay → file case within 1 month. CTS: moving to continuous clearing (since Oct 2025).
  • MICR Code (9 digits at bottom of cheque): First 3 = City | Middle 3 = Bank Code | Last 3 = Branch Code.
  • Demand Draft (DD): Prepaid bank order; valid 3 months; cash limit < ₹50,000 (≥ ₹50,000 via cheque + PAN).
  • Cards & Terms: Debit Card = 16 digits | CVV = Card Verification Value (proves physical possession) | EMV = Europay, Mastercard, Visa | MDR = Merchant Discount Rate | Charge Card (pay in full or pay fees).
  • Loans, Gold LTV (Eff. 1 Apr 2026) & Sec 80C: Mortgage = loan against immovable property (flat/house). FITL = Funded Interest Term Loan. RBI Gold Loan Tiered LTV: 85% (≤ ₹2.5L), 80% (> ₹2.5L–₹5L), 75% (> ₹5L). Sec 80C max deduction = ₹1.5 Lakh/FY (PPF, SSY, ELSS, NSC, 5-yr FD, SCSS — excludes KVP).
Cheque Crossing Trap A plain Crossed Cheque means "collect through a bank account" (NOT "cannot be endorsed"). "Cannot be endorsed" applies ONLY to an Account Payee crossing.

RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (RB-IOS)

  • History & Motto ("One Nation, One Ombudsman"): Statutory base: Section 35A, BR Act 1949. Originated 1995 → 2002 → 2006 (merged Banking 2006 + NBFC 2018 + Digital Transactions 2019 into RB-IOS 2021 on 12 Nov 2021; CICs added 2022) → 2026 Scheme (took effect June/1 July 2026). Zero filing fee; financial loss not the only criteria.
  • Compensation Limits (Paper vs 2026): 2021 Scheme: Consequential loss up to ₹20 Lakh + harassment up to ₹1 Lakh. 2026 Scheme: Underlying dispute value no limit; consequential loss up to ₹30 Lakh + harassment up to ₹3 Lakh.
  • Covered Entities & Tenure: All SCBs, RRBs, Cooperative Banks (non-scheduled UCBs if deposits ≥ ₹50 Cr), customer-interface NBFCs (assets > ₹100 Cr) & deposit-taking NBFCs, Non-bank PPI issuers, CICs. Excluded: Housing Finance Companies (HFCs). Ombudsman/Deputy tenure = 3 years (21 Regional Offices).
  • Filing, Channels & Proceedings: Complain to bank first → wait 30 days → file via CMS or CRPC (Chandigarh) within 90 days (not via RBI Contact Centre; advocates normally barred). Summary proceedings; RE gets 15 days to respond (else ex-parte). Accept award within 30 days. Appeal within 30 days to ED in charge of CEPD. Principal Nodal Officer ≥ GM rank; salient features in English, Hindi & Regional.

Customer Rights, NARCL & International Tax Reporting

  • Charter of Customer Rights (5 Rights): Fair treatment; Transparency, fair & honest dealing; Suitability; Privacy; Grievance redress & compensation (promoted by BCSBI).
  • FATCA & CRS: FATCA (Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act) = US law (2010) requiring foreign banks to report US taxpayers (India signed IGA in 2015). CRS (Common Reporting Standard) = global automatic tax-info exchange by OECD.
  • NARCL & DICGC 90-Day Payout: NARCL = India's Bad Bank (stressed asset resolution). Under DICGC (Amendment) Act, 2021, depositors receive up to ₹5 Lakh within max 90 days of moratorium (each bank insured separately).
